# Beautiful in Its Time: The Artistry of God and the Eternity in Our Hearts

*"He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also set eternity in the human heart; yet no one can fathom what God has done from beginning to end."* — Ecclesiastes 3:11

Hear that verse slowly, and you will find it holds three claims in creative tension. God makes all things beautiful — but only in their proper time. God has planted eternity within us — we carry an infinite longing in our finite frames. And yet we cannot fully comprehend His ways — the mystery remains. This is not a verse about optimism. It is a verse about the nature of reality itself. And if we let it, it will change how we see the ugly season, the long waiting, the grief, the unanswered question.

## Beauty Ordered, Not Scattered

Notice first what the Preacher does not say. He does not say God makes everything pleasant, or painless, or immediately understandable. He says God makes everything *beautiful in its time*. God does not scatter beauty randomly. He orders it. He appoints it to a time.

No one in our tradition was more captivated by beauty and time than Augustine, who cried out in his *Confessions*: "Late have I loved you, beauty so old and so new: late have I loved you." For Augustine, God is not merely beautiful — God *is* Beauty itself, the eternal source from which all temporal beauty flows. And time itself is a creature, something God made, while God stands outside it in an eternal present: "Thy years are one day; and Thy day is not daily, but Today... Thy Today is Eternity."

Here is why that matters for you. We suffer because we experience time as a stream — one moment at a time. God sees the entire river at once. What appears broken, ugly, or disordered in our narrow slice of history may in fact be part of a larger tapestry of beauty that only God can see in its totality. The grief of a season, the apparent waste of a life, the waiting — all of it is beautiful in its time, because the One who stands in eternal simultaneity has already beheld its completion.

## The Cosmic Curriculum

Go deeper still, as Origen of Alexandria taught us to do, beneath the surface of the words. "Everything beautiful in its time" is not a platitude about seasons. It is a declaration about the divine economy of salvation. History itself is a vast educational drama in which God is patiently drawing His creatures back to Himself. Every age, every suffering, every mercy has its appointed beauty within this grand arc of restoration. Creation was subjected to vanity not willingly, but in hope; it awaits the fulfillment of a promise.

So when you encounter true beauty in time — in a face, a sunset, a kindness, a moment of grief resolved — you are encountering a trace of the eternal curriculum, a lesson from God written into creation itself. Nothing in your life is filler. Every season is a page in the syllabus of a God who wastes nothing.

## The Ladder of Beauty

But beauty is not only a lesson to be learned; it is a summons to climb. Gregory of Nyssa saw the soul's life as *epektasis* — a perpetual, never-ending ascent toward God. "Hope always draws the soul from the beauty which is seen to what is beyond," he wrote, "always kindles the desire for the hidden through what is constantly perceived."

Every beautiful thing in time is not the destination — it is an icon, a pointer. The beauty God makes in each moment is like a rung on an infinite ladder. We are meant to see it, be moved by it, and then reach beyond it toward the eternal Beauty from which it came. A child's laughter, a season changing, a prayer answered — each is God drawing your soul higher and deeper. Every beautiful moment is a divine lure.

And Gregory gives us two sentences worth carrying for a lifetime: "Concepts create idols; only wonder grasps anything." And: "The soul that looks on beauty becomes beautiful." This is why the second half of the verse — *no one can fathom what God has done from beginning to end* — is not a discouragement. God is always infinitely more than we can grasp. The mystery is not a wall; it is an open horizon that calls us to keep ascending.

## The Author of Order

Thomas Aquinas would add the metaphysical ground beneath all this. Beauty, for him, is a transcendental property of Being itself, alongside Truth and Goodness — whatever exists, insofar as it exists, participates in beauty. And beauty has three conditions: *integritas*, wholeness; *consonantia*, harmony and proportion; *claritas*, radiance.

When Ecclesiastes says God has made everything beautiful in its time, it declares that God is the author of order itself. Each created thing has its proper form, its proper proportion, its proper time — and when it exists in its fullness, it radiates the glory of its Creator. Even the formless and void of Genesis — *tohu wa-bohu* — is only the absence of form and beauty, not their negation. God moves over the chaos and brings forth order, proportion, and radiance. He is still doing it, in the chaos of your own life.

Aquinas said, "The things that we love tell us what we are." What we call beautiful tells us what God has placed deep within us: a resonance with His own nature. We recognize beauty because we carry His image.

## The Eternity Sewn into Your Heart

Now the second clause, which may be the most staggering line in all of Ecclesiastes: *"He has also set eternity in the human heart."*

The Hebrew word is *olam* — eternity, the ages, the world. God has stitched a sense of the infinite into our finite hearts. We instinctively know there is more than what we see. This is the great wound of humanity — and its greatest glory. We are time-bound creatures who nonetheless ache for the eternal. No earthly beauty fully satisfies. Augustine knew it: "our hearts are restless until they rest in You." Gregory knew it: the soul always wants more of God.

This is why beautiful things can make us sad — why a perfect sunset or a child's joy can bring tears. We sense, beneath the beauty, that it is passing — and we long for a beauty that does not pass away. Do not despise that ache. That longing is itself the mark of the divine in you. Every beautiful moment in time whispers of a beauty that does not fade.

## Take This With You

So what shall we do with this verse? Believe what it actually says: there is no moment, however dark or confusing, that falls outside the artistry of God. The ugly season, the long waiting, the grief, the unanswered question — all of it is in the hands of the One who stands in eternity and whose deepest name is Beautiful.

Let every beauty you meet this week be a rung, not a resting place. Let the sunset move you, and then let it move you *upward*. Let the ache it leaves behind remind you that you were not made for passing things. Because He has placed eternity in your heart, you will never be fully at rest in any created beauty — and that is mercy, not cruelty. You were made for the Source.

And when your own season feels formless and void, remember: that is not the negation of beauty, only its not-yet. The Spirit still moves over chaos. He has made everything beautiful — in its time. Wait for it. Look for it. And let the soul that looks on beauty become beautiful.
