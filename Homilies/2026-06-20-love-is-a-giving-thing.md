# The Spring That Flows Outward: Love as Self-Donation

"Love is a giving thing." Say it slowly, and it cuts to the very marrow of both Christian theology and deep human experience. We spend so much of our lives asking what love can get us — comfort, belonging, someone to hold. But love, in its truest form, does not ask *"what can I get?"* It continuously asks *"what can I give?"* And when we follow that question all the way down, we find it does not end in a feeling or a virtue. It ends in God Himself.

## Love That Pours Itself Out

Paul the Apostle gave us perhaps the most penetrating description of love ever written. Notice that in his great love chapter, every quality he names is outward-facing and self-emptying:

> *"Love is patient and kind; love does not envy or boast; it is not arrogant or rude... Love bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things."* — 1 Corinthians 13

This is **agape** — the Greek word Paul uses for divine, self-giving love. It is not a transaction. It is a pouring-out. And Paul's deepest point is that this love is not merely something God *does* — it is the nature of God Himself, expressed most fully in the Cross:

> *"God demonstrates His own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."* — Romans 5:8

Here is the staggering logic: God did not wait until we were lovable. He gave Himself when we were at our worst. Love, in its truest form, **gives first, gives freely, and gives without guarantee of return.** That is why Paul can conclude, *"And now these three remain: faith, hope and love. But the greatest of these is love."* Love is the greatest because it is the most Godlike thing a human being can do.

## Gift-Love and Need-Love

C.S. Lewis, in *The Four Loves*, names the two fundamental movements within our loving. There is **Need-Love** — "I love you because I need you." This is not shameful; it is how a child loves a parent, and we never outgrow it entirely. But there is also **Gift-Love** — "I love you and I give to you, desiring nothing in return."

Gift-Love, Lewis writes, is the closest human beings can come to imitating God, because it asks nothing for itself. It flows outward like a spring — giving, nourishing, sustaining — without needing to be replenished from the one it loves.

And then Lewis makes his most profound move: even our capacity for Gift-Love is itself *given to us*. We cannot manufacture this love on our own. Scripture says it plainly:

> *"Not that we loved God, but that He loved us first."* — 1 John 4:19

So "love is a giving thing" is true at every level. Love gives kindness, patience, and sacrifice. Love gives even when it costs something. And God gives love to us so that we might give it to others. We are not the source of the spring; we are channels dug by grace.

## God *Is* Love

The Apostle John makes the most stunning declaration in all of Scripture:

> *"God is love."* — 1 John 4:8

Notice he does not say "God is loving" — an attribute among others. He says God **is** love — an identity. Love is the very essence of God's being; everything God does flows from it. But do not mistake this for a sentimental claim. It does not mean God is merely a warm, tolerant force, or that love is defined by our emotions, or that God cannot judge and correct. A love that is God's own nature is holy fire, not vague affirmation.

The Trinity is the deepest key here. God is not lonely — He has eternally been a community of love: Father giving to Son, Son to Father, in the Spirit. **Love didn't begin when God created us.** Love is what God has always been *within Himself*. Creation, then, was God's love overflowing outward — giving existence to what had none, giving beauty, giving life. Before anything was created, God was already an eternal act of giving. The Cross is that same love made visible in time — God giving Himself completely, holding nothing back.

## Called Home to the Source

Bring it all together, and the phrase becomes a confession of faith. Love is a giving thing because its very nature is self-donation. God is love because self-donation is what He eternally is. And we love because we were first loved — we are recipients of a giving that preceded us and sustains us.

So here is the exhortation: stop measuring your love by what it earns you. Give first. Give freely. Give without guarantee of return — to the difficult neighbor, the ungrateful child, the friend who cannot repay you. Every such act joins you to the eternal current of Father, Son, and Spirit. And when you cannot summon the love, remember that you were never its source. Ask for it, and let the spring flow through you.

Lewis observed, *"If I find in myself a desire which no experience in this world can satisfy, the most probable explanation is that I was made for another world."* That ache you feel when love is at its most beautiful — that is the echo of the God who **is** love, calling you home to the source of all giving. Go home to Him. And then go out and give.
