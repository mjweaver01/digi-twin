# Castling

Castling is a special move involving your **King** and one of your **Rooks**. It's the only move where you move two pieces at once, and every game you should try to do it — it keeps your King safe and connects your Rooks.

## How It Works

The King slides **2 squares** toward a Rook, and that Rook jumps to the other side of the King.

There are two types:
- **Kingside castling** (short castling) — King goes to g1, Rook lands on f1
- **Queenside castling** (long castling) — King goes to c1, Rook lands on d1

*(For Black, same idea but mirrored: g8/f8 kingside, c8/d8 queenside)*

## When You CAN'T Castle

You lose the right to castle if **any** of these are true:
1. The King has already moved
2. That specific Rook has already moved
3. There are pieces between the King and Rook
4. Your King is currently in **check**
5. Your King would **pass through** a square that's under attack
6. Your King would **land on** a square that's under attack

## Why It Matters

- Tucks your King behind a wall of pawns — much safer than sitting in the center
- Activates your Rook by moving it toward the center
- **Rule of thumb**: Castle within the first 10 moves if you can

## Tips

- Kingside castling is more common — it's faster (fewer pieces to move out of the way)
- Don't delay castling just to attack — an uncastled King is a liability
- If you can't castle, at least move your King to a corner manually ("artificial castling")
