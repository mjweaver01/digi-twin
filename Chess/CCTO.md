# CCTO — Candidate Move Checklist

CCTO is a simple mental checklist to run through **before every move**. It stops you from missing tactics, blundering into threats, and playing aimlessly. The idea: scan these four categories in order, and you'll rarely overlook something important.

> **CCTO** = **C**hecks · **C**aptures · **T**hreats · **O**thers

---

## Why It Works

Most blunders happen not because a player couldn't calculate — but because they never *looked* at the right move in the first place. CCTO forces you to consider the forcing moves first (checks and captures), then threats, before settling on a quiet positional move. Forcing moves are often the best moves, and they're the easiest to overlook when you're focused on your own plan.

---

## The Four Categories

### C — Checks
Look at every check you can give. Not all of them will be good, but some will win material, force a King to a worse square, or even deliver checkmate.

- Can I give check with my Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight?
- Does giving check force my opponent's King somewhere useful?
- Is there a *discovered check* hiding in the position?

Even if no check is good right now, knowing you *have* checks keeps options open.

---

### C — Captures
Look at every capture available to you. Ask: what happens if I take that piece?

- Can I capture something for free (a hanging piece)?
- Can I capture and win material after recaptures?
- Does a capture open a file, diagonal, or rank I can use?

Don't just look at your captures — look at your opponent's too. Ask: "What can they capture next move?"

---

### T — Threats
What threats can you *create* with your move? A threat forces your opponent to react, giving you the initiative.

- Can I attack an undefended piece?
- Can I create a fork, pin, or skewer?
- Can I threaten checkmate — even one they can defend — to force their hand?
- What is *my opponent* threatening right now? (Always answer this first.)

> **Tip:** Before making any move, ask "What is my opponent threatening?" If you can't answer that, stop and look harder.

---

### O — Others
If no check, capture, or threat is compelling, consider positional "other" moves:

- Improve your **least active piece** (see [[Capablanca's Principles]])
- Advance a **passed pawn**
- Place a **Rook on an open file**
- Strengthen your **King safety**
- Improve your **pawn structure**

"Others" isn't a fallback for lazy thinking — it's where most positional chess lives. But you should only arrive here *after* ruling out forcing moves.

---

## Using CCTO at the Board

A practical rhythm:

1. **Opponent moves** → immediately ask "What are they threatening?"
2. **Scan Checks** — any checks available to me?
3. **Scan Captures** — any captures available to me?
4. **Scan Threats** — what threat can I create?
5. **If none are good** → think positionally (Others)
6. **Before moving** → run the check one more time on your chosen move: does it blunder anything?

You don't need to calculate every line — just *look* at each category. Most of the time, step 6 (the blunder check) is what saves you.

---

## Common Mistakes CCTO Prevents

- **Missing a free piece** — if you scanned Captures, you won't leave pieces hanging
- **Walking into a checkmate** — if you asked "what are they threatening?", you'll see it
- **Playing without a plan** — Others forces you to think about *why* you're making a quiet move
- **Forgetting discovered attacks** — Checks reminds you to look for them
