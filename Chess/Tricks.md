# Chess Tricks & Tactics

Tactics are short sequences of moves that win material or deliver checkmate. Chess at the beginner level is mostly decided by tactics — whoever spots them first wins. Learn these patterns and your rating will jump.

> **Tip:** Six motifs cover most beginner tactics — **Fork, Pin, Skewer, Discovered Attack, Overloaded Piece, Back Rank**. Scan for each of them before every move.

---

## Core Tactical Motifs

### 1. Fork
One piece attacks **two or more enemy pieces** at the same time. The opponent can only save one.

- **Knight fork** is the most common — Knights are sneaky because they jump over pieces
- Example: A Knight on e5 can fork a King on g6 and a Queen on c6 simultaneously

**Watch for:** Knights near your King and Queen at the same time.

---

### 2. Pin
A piece is "pinned" when moving it would expose a more valuable piece behind it to attack.

- **Absolute pin**: The piece behind is the King — you literally *cannot* move the pinned piece
- **Relative pin**: The piece behind is valuable (Queen, Rook) but not the King — moving is legal but costly

Example: Bishop on b5 pins a Knight on c6 to the King on e8. The Knight can't move without putting the King in check.

---

### 3. Skewer
The **opposite of a pin** — a valuable piece is attacked, and when it moves, a lesser piece behind it is captured.

Example: Rook attacks the enemy Queen. Queen moves. Rook captures the Rook (or minor piece) behind it.

---

### 4. Discovered Attack
Moving one piece **reveals an attack** from another piece behind it. Extremely powerful because the opponent has to deal with two threats.

- **Discovered check** = moving a piece reveals a check from behind — very hard to handle
- **Double check** = the moving piece *also* gives check — the only legal reply is to move the King

---

### 5. Overloaded Piece
A piece that is defending **two things at once** — if you attack both, it can't protect both.

Example: A Rook defending both a Knight and a back-rank mate threat. Attack the Knight and the Rook has to choose.

---

### 6. Back Rank Weakness
If your King is trapped behind a row of pawns with no escape square, a Rook or Queen on the 8th rank (or 1st for White) can deliver checkmate.

**Fix it:** Move a pawn one square (h3 or g3) to give your King an escape square. This is called "giving the King luft" (air).

## Famous Beginner Traps

### Scholar's Mate (4-move checkmate)
```
1.e4 e5  2.Bc4 Nc6  3.Qh5 Nf6??  4.Qxf7#
```
White's Queen and Bishop team up on f7 — the weakest square near Black's King early in the game.

**How to stop it:** As Black, play `3...g6` to chase the Queen, or `3...Qe7` to defend f7.

> Don't rely on Scholar's Mate yourself — experienced players know it and you'll waste your Queen.

### Fried Liver Attack
After the Italian Game, White sacrifices a Knight on f7 to blow open Black's King. Very aggressive but requires specific move order. Worth knowing so you don't fall for it.

## How to Get Better at Tactics

- **Puzzle training** every day — Chess.com and Lichess.org both have free puzzle trainers
- Even 10–15 puzzles a day will dramatically improve your pattern recognition
- Look for tactics in your own games *after* they're over (game analysis)
