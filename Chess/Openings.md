# Chess Openings

The opening is the first phase of the game (roughly moves 1–15). You don't need to memorize dozens of lines — just follow solid principles and you'll be fine as a beginner.

## The 4 Golden Rules of the Opening

1. **Control the center** — Move pawns to e4/d4 (as White) or e5/d5 (as Black). The center is the most powerful area of the board.
2. **Develop your pieces** — Get your Knights and Bishops off the back rank as quickly as possible. Developed pieces = more options.
3. **Castle early** — Get your King to safety. See [[Castling]].
4. **Don't move the same piece twice** — Unless you have to. Every tempo matters early.

### What to Avoid
- Don't bring your Queen out too early — it can get chased around and you'll lose time
- Don't move too many pawns — pawns don't develop pieces
- Don't put pieces on the edge of the board (Knights on a/h files are weak)

## Common Beginner-Friendly Openings

### As White

**Italian Game** (very recommended for beginners)
```
1. e4   e5
2. Nf3  Nc6
3. Bc4
```
Simple, logical, develops fast, aims at f7 (a weak pawn near Black's King).

**London System**
```
1. d4   d5
2. Nf3  Nf6
3. Bf4
```
Very solid, easy to learn. Same setup works against almost anything Black plays.

---

### As Black

**Responding to 1.e4:**
- **1...e5** — Mirror White, fight for the center. Leads to open, tactical games.
- **1...c6** (Caro-Kann) — Solid and positional. See [[Caro-Kann]].

**Responding to 1.d4:**
- **1...d5** — Solid and symmetrical.
- **1...Nf6** — Flexible, leads to many systems.
