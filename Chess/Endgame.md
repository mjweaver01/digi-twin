# The Endgame

The endgame begins when most pieces have been traded off and the board is relatively bare. Endgames feel slower, but they're incredibly precise — small mistakes can throw away a won position. Many beginners neglect endgames; learning them is a big edge.

---

## The King Wakes Up

In the opening and middlegame, your King hides. In the endgame, **your King is a powerful piece** — use it aggressively. Centralize it immediately.

A King in the center controls up to 8 squares and helps push pawns, support your pieces, and harass the enemy King.

---

## Essential Endgame Concepts

### Opposition
When two Kings face each other with one square between them, the player who does *not* have to move has the **opposition** — and that's an advantage. The opponent's King must give way.

Example: White King on e3, Black King on e5. It's Black's turn — Black is in opposition and must step aside. White's King can advance.

This matters hugely in King + Pawn vs. King endgames.

---

### The Square Rule (Rule of the Square)
Without doing calculation, you can tell if a King can catch a passer pawn:

1. Draw an imaginary square from the pawn to the promotion square
2. If the opposing King can step **into** that square on their turn, they can catch the pawn
3. If not, the pawn queens

---

### Passed Pawns
A pawn with no enemy pawns able to stop it (on its file or adjacent files) is **passed**. These are endgame gold.

> "A passed pawn is a criminal that should be kept under lock and key." — Nimzowitsch

Push them! Every step closer to promotion is a threat that forces your opponent to react.

---

## Basic Checkmates to Know

### King + Queen vs. King
Use the Queen to shrink the enemy King's space, then bring your own King in to assist. The enemy King gets cornered and checkmated.

Pattern: Force King to edge → bring your King close → deliver checkmate

### King + Rook vs. King
Cut the enemy King off with the Rook ("lawnmower" technique — Rook on a rank, push King back, then march your King over).

### King + 2 Bishops vs. King
Possible but requires technique. Force the King to a corner with matching-color diagonals.

### King + Bishop + Knight vs. King
Very difficult — requires precise technique. Don't worry about this one as a beginner.

---

## Pawn Endgames

### King + Pawn vs. King
The most fundamental endgame. The key question: can the attacking King escort the pawn to promotion?

- If the King is **in front** of its pawn, it usually wins (with opposition)
- If the King is **behind** its pawn, it's trickier
- Edge pawns (a and h files) are often drawn even when you're ahead — no room to maneuver

### Zugzwang
A position where **whoever has to move loses** (or is put in a worse position). Common in King + Pawn endgames. Occurs when there are no good moves — any move makes things worse.

---

## Endgame Tips for Beginners

1. **Activate your King immediately** — don't leave it on g1 once pieces come off
2. **Know the basic checkmates** — practice K+Q vs K and K+R vs K until they're automatic
3. **Create a passed pawn** or stop your opponent's passed pawn
4. **Rook endgames are the most common** — Rooks belong *behind* passed pawns (your own and the enemy's)
5. **Don't rush** — trades that simplify to a won endgame are good; trades into a drawn endgame are mistakes
